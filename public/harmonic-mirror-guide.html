Understand the chords you're playing.

The live Harmonic Mirror identifies the notes coming from your MIDI keyboard and helps you understand chord names, chord tones, interval relationships, voice movement and musical function. Each view focuses on one part of your playing.

Chords

Which chord am I playing?

The chord name appears in the center of the live display. The individual sounding notes remain visible around it. The chord reader uses the notes you play and the selected home key to choose a name when it has an exact match. When multiple names are possible, harmonic context and bass position help determine which reading makes sense.

CEG
C major

Root, major third, perfect fifth.

CEGB
Cmaj7

A major triad plus its major seventh.

CEGBD
Cmaj9

A major seventh chord with an added ninth.

Playing the same chord tones in different octaves usually keeps the same basic chord name. A different bass note may give the chord a slash-chord name, such as C/E.

Intervals

What are the distances between the chord tones?

The chord root is the starting point. The other notes are shown in relation to it. The numbers tell you the distance in semitones.

C · RootE · +4G · +7

In C major, E is four semitones above C, and G is seven semitones above C. Different third and fifth intervals change the quality of a chord.

This view shows pitch-class intervals. Exact octaves are still received from your MIDI keyboard.

Voice movement

How did I get from one chord to the next?

The previous and current chords are shown separately. Notes that stayed exactly the same are connected directly. Moving notes show their change in semitones.

G → G · heldB → B · heldD → C · −2F → E · −1

For example, G7 can move to Cmaj7/G by holding G and B, moving D down to C and moving F down to E.

The app estimates which notes connect most closely. Complex voicings can have more than one musically reasonable assignment.

Key relationships

What does this chord mean in different keys?

A chord's notes can stay the same while its harmonic function changes. Select your home key, then compare other key centers to understand where the same chord fits.

Am7
In C major: vi7

A minor seventh chord built on the sixth degree.

Am7
In G major: ii7

The second-degree minor seventh chord.

Am7
In F major: iii7

The third-degree minor seventh chord.

A–C–E–G remains Am7 in all three examples. The musical key and surrounding progression help determine its function; scale-note overlap alone does not mean a modulation has occurred.

Playing with MIDI

What does Harmonic Mirror receive from my keyboard?

The app tracks the exact MIDI notes across A0–C8, including note-on, note-off, velocity and sustain-pedal behavior. Chord names update as the notes change. When several pitches remain ringing under sustain, the app considers them part of the sounding harmony until released.

Not every unusual cluster has one unambiguous chord name. If an exact identification isn't available, Harmonic Mirror says so instead of inventing one.
