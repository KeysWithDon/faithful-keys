What you are seeing

Each note has a fixed location. All twelve major/natural minor key centers surround all twelve pitch-class notes. Exactly seven membership strands leave each key; each inner note is shared by seven keys. The same scale pattern is transposed to each key; its shape rotates, rather than being randomly reassigned. As you play chords, note relationships light up, and their trails become an artwork of your progression.

  • Try it now: click “Gospel 2–5–1” or play MIDI into a supported desktop browser.
  • Shape logic: each key glyph is generated from the seven tones of its major (or natural minor) scale. Every chord creates edges between the actual notes played. Register creates the height dimension.
  • Color logic: each clockwise fifth adds exactly 30 degrees on the hue wheel. There is no objectively correct sound-to-visible-color mapping.
  • Cycle logic: the center has six fixed interval-class patterns. Their lobe counts equal 12 / gcd(12, interval class). Only cycles present between sounding chord tones light up.
  • Oscilloscope logic: the fixed network remains visible as animated traces run along its active musical connections. Fundamental MIDI pitch controls time-compressed visual phase rate, interval cycle length controls spatial oscillations, note velocity controls wave amplitude, and sustain keeps an active signal illuminated. This is generated from MIDI data, not recorded acoustic waveform samples.
  • Key-note net: each of the 84 always-visible strands represents a real scale membership. Select Home and Compare keys: shared tones illuminate both routes to the same note. These are diatonic scale memberships, not every possible chromatic note over a jazz chord.
  • Voice tracking: precise MIDI pitches (including octave) are compared across successive note sets, preserving common notes and tracing changed voices by minimum semitone movement. A different assignment may be plausible when voices cross.
  • Choose home key: the map is not rearranged when you change keys. Only the harmonic interpretation changes.
  • Learn: see the inferred chord, its functional role, and destination suggestions. Chord interpretation is contextual and can be ambiguous for rootless or incomplete voicings.
  • Build art: trails accumulate until “Clear trails.” Export PNG or a JSON session.
  • 88 exact lights: Every piano note A0–C8 occupies a permanent individual neuron, grouped by register. Shared pitch-class hubs connect all octaves, and semitone (+1), fifth (+7), and octave (+12) fibers have literal interval meanings.
  • Voice movement: When a voice moves, the bright path connects the exact old and new register lights while common tones remain stationary.
  • Cymatic-inspired: the patterns visualize pitch-class interval cycles, not laboratory measurements of vibrating sand, membranes, or Chladni plates. The term is a mathematical visual analogy, not a physical reconstruction.
